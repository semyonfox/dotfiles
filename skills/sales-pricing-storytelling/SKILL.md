## Core frame

Sales is clarity around value, proof, buyer path, and price model. Not volume. Not generic “unlock your potential” sludge.

A price is not one number. It is a puzzle of segment, metric, package, anchor, and proof.

## Pricing workflow

1. Identify buyer segments.
2. For each segment, write the value metric they actually care about.
3. Choose a price metric that tracks value perception: per seat, per usage, per org, per outcome, per project, etc.
4. Package around buyer needs, not internal feature lists.
5. Anchor against alternatives, cost of doing nothing, manual labour, risk, or higher tiers.
6. Add proof that makes the promised value believable.

## Segmentation prompts

Ask:

- Who feels this pain most often?
- Who has budget or authority?
- Who gets personal credit for solving it?
- What do they currently pay with: money, time, risk, embarrassment, admin effort?
- What would make the offer obviously worth it?

## Story structure

Use narrative when facts alone are not landing:

1. Specific person/problem.
2. Conflict or cost.
3. Change after product/service.
4. Proof.
5. Ask.

Lead with the world the buyer recognizes, then show the product.

## Copy review: three questions

From the AI-copy talk:

- **Concern:** what worry/blocker does the user have here?
- **Human:** what would a helpful human say at this exact moment?
- **POV:** is this written from the user’s perspective or the company’s org chart?

## Sales page checklist

- [ ] Hero states buyer pain and outcome.
- [ ] Target segment is obvious.
- [ ] Benefits map to real pains.
- [ ] Pricing metric matches value metric.
- [ ] Packages have clear buyer logic.
- [ ] Price is anchored.
- [ ] Objections are handled before they stop the buyer.
- [ ] CTA says what happens next.
- [ ] Proof is specific: screenshots, numbers, examples, demo, testimonials.

## Red flags

- One flat package for buyer segments with different value perception.
- Pricing hidden because the team is scared, not because enterprise sales truly requires it.
- Benefits like “save time” with no concrete scene or proof.
- AI copy that sounds polished but answers no user concern.
- Feature list before problem clarity.
