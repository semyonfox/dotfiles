# Irish LTD compliance research notes

Use this when answering Irish LTD formation, Revenue/CRO/RBO, tax-registration, annual-return, VAT/PAYE, or accountant-vs-DIY questions.

## Source priority

Prefer official/current sources first:

1. Revenue Commissioners (`revenue.ie`) for tax registration, ROS, CT, VAT, PAYE, preliminary tax, iXBRL, filing/payment obligations.
2. Companies Registration Office (`cro.ie`) for incorporation forms, fees, B1 annual returns, CORE, financial statement attachment rules, late filing penalties, audit-exemption consequences.
3. Central Register of Beneficial Ownership (`rbo.gov.ie`) for RBO filing duties, beneficial owner details, deadlines, PPSN/VIF identity requirements.
4. Secondary sources only for interpretation or practical colour. Label them as secondary and do not use them to override official pages.

## User-specific pattern from session

When Semyon asks "are you 100% sure" or challenges a compliance claim, do not rely on broad summaries. Pull current official pages, quote the exact wording that supports the answer, include the page title, source organisation, URL, and any visible published/updated date.

For Irish company compliance specifically, distinguish clearly between:

- **Legal possibility**: whether the official source allows the company/director to do it without an accountant/tax agent.
- **Practical recommendation**: whether DIY is sensible given complexity.

Example phrasing:

> Revenue explicitly distinguishes between a company represented by a tax agent and one not represented by a tax agent, so an accountant/tax agent is not mandatory for that step. That does not mean DIY is wise for CT1/accounts/VAT.

## Official facts verified in June 2026 session

Revenue, "Registering for tax", published 04 June 2026:

- Businesses must register for tax when starting a new company.
- If a company is represented by a tax agent, the agent submits online through ROS.
- If the company is not represented by a tax agent, it submits Form TR2 for Irish resident companies or TR2(FT) for foreign companies.

Revenue, "How to register for tax as a new company", published 04 June 2026:

- A CRO number is required first.
- "When you start a new company, you or your tax agent must inform Revenue."
- If not represented by an agent, use Form TR2.
- TR2 can register for Corporation Tax, Employer PAYE, VAT, and RCT.
- Once registered, all payments and returns must be filed online through ROS.

Revenue, "Mandatory electronic payment of taxes and filing of tax returns", published 04 June 2026:

- ROS lets business customers pay liabilities, file returns, access tax details, and claim repayments.
- Most Revenue business customers are legally obliged to e-file and e-pay; non-use can attract penalties.

Revenue, "Corporation Tax (CT) payment and filing", published 01 October 2025:

- Irish resident companies and certain non-resident companies pay CT on taxable profits.
- The section explains how companies pay CT and file tax returns.

Revenue, "Preliminary Corporation Tax (CT)", published 01 October 2025:

- New/start-up companies do not have to pay preliminary tax for the first accounting period if CT is under €200,000, excluding surcharge but including Section 239 income tax. Instead they pay final CT when submitting the CT return.

CRO, "Filing an Annual Return":

- Form B1 must be delivered electronically by a company, whether trading or not, at least once yearly through CORE.
- First annual return is due exactly six months after incorporation and does not require financial statements.
- Annual returns are mandatory e-filings, including financial statements and electronic payment where required.

## Answering guidance

For legal/tax topics:

- Include a short non-advice disclaimer, but do not hide behind it.
- Give a direct answer first.
- Use official quote-backed bullets for challenged claims.
- State the publication/update date if visible.
- Separate "can do" from "should do".
- Warn about traps: CT1 complexity, iXBRL, VAT/digital services, payroll/PAYE, director loans, audit exemption loss from late B1, and needing a statutory auditor if audit exemption is unavailable.
