# International work-placement search notes — Semyon

Use when coaching Semyon on university work placement / internship searches where the placement should be more than a module requirement.

## Hard constraints

- Placement target: January start, roughly 8–9 months, ending Aug/Sep.
- It must satisfy University of Galway work-placement requirements; verify duration and approval rules before recommending roles.
- 10–13 week internships and summer-only roles are usually noise unless explicitly framed as prestige fallbacks.
- 6-month roles are only useful if the placement office accepts them or the employer can extend.

## Preference shape

Semyon does not want a generic safe local placement list unless asked for safety options. He wants a placement that is a pivot-point life experience: strong brand, serious engineering, international city, high learning rate, and a story worth telling.

Preferred opportunity classes:

- Big-name / elite tech: Cloudflare, Datadog, AWS/Amazon, Microsoft, Stripe, Apple, Google, Meta, Netflix, Twitch, Databricks, Nvidia, OpenAI/Anthropic where realistic.
- High-quality EU tech hubs: Lisbon, London, Paris, Amsterdam, Berlin, Munich, Madrid, Barcelona, Dublin, Cork, Eindhoven.
- Top-tier infra/AI/devtools/security/data startups can count if they are high-signal and ownership-heavy; random startups should not be treated as equivalent to big-name placements.

Avoid Russia and too-eastern Europe due to personal/geopolitical/military-service concerns.

Language assumptions:

- English ideal.
- Conversational French can be tuned.
- Basic German/Spanish/Portuguese; a few Italian words.

## Search framing

Start broad and aspirational, then classify by compliance:

1. Strict fit: Jan–Aug/Sep, 8–9 months, placement/co-op, SWE/backend/platform/DevOps/cloud.
2. Negotiable fit: 5–6 month elite EU internship where extension or university approval may be possible.
3. Prestige fallback: short Big Tech internship that is life/CV valuable but does not satisfy placement by itself.
4. Safety net: local/standard placement; include separately and do not let it dominate the search.

For US roles, explicitly check J-1 Intern feasibility. Ask whether the host supports J-1 interns through a designated sponsor for off-cycle university placements. If a role says existing US work authorization/no sponsorship only, mark it high-friction or infeasible.

## Style notes from the session

- Do not respond with a long generic list of standard Galway/Ireland placements when Semyon asks for ambitious damage-control or life-experience opportunities.
- He may use frustration/profanity to steer scope; treat it as useful signal, not a tone problem.
- Be blunt about market reality, but keep momentum: “Big Tech rarely does 8-month Jan placements; the play is to hunt off-cycle flexibility and ask recruiters directly.”
- Separate “module-compliant” from “prestige/life-experience” instead of mixing them in one undifferentiated table.

## Useful recruiter ask

> I’m a Computer Science & IT student at University of Galway looking for my compulsory full-time work placement from January to August/September 2027. I’m targeting software engineering, backend, platform, DevOps/SRE, or cloud roles. Does [Company] support off-cycle university placements of around 8 months, particularly in [city/team]?

For US:

> As an Irish/EU student, I would likely need a J-1 Intern route through a designated sponsor. Does your team support J-1 interns for off-cycle university placements?
