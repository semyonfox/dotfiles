# Seán’s career-reset advice

Source: a private Discord conversation, July 2026. Preserve as practical peer advice, not universal law or a claim about every company.

## The advice worth keeping

- **There is no irrelevant experience.** A job, technology, or domain may not be where you stay, but doing it once gives you durable judgement and context. “Maybe you’ll never go that low level again, but if you went there once, you’ll never forget it.”
- **Diversify deliberately.** Real opportunities often come from adjacent experiences rather than a single narrowly optimised track. Seán’s own return to farming was an example of a useful reset, not a career failure.
- **When a company’s centre of gravity shifts away from you, bail early.** His blunt heuristic: if the founder/boss is backing away and you are clearly not part of what follows, leave “hard and fast.” It is a signal to investigate, not an automatic rule.
- **Burnout is not fixed by maximising the application grind.** Pause the compulsive applications/automation when they are melting your brain; regain energy and momentum first.
- **Build things and talk to people.** Attend local events (Galway, Dublin, PorterShed, inter-society), meet people, and stay in touch. High-calibre/fun opportunities tend to arrive through visible work plus real relationships, not only cold applications.
- **Make playful work, not only portfolio SaaS.** Build small games, do game jams, make silly web apps, or use a strange API because it is fun. This develops breadth, creates interesting stories, and makes it easier to meet collaborators.
- **Keep the serious work, remove the pressure.** Keep the startup moving, but do not demand every side project become a startup, portfolio bullet, or placement strategy. A small project like Seol can be worthwhile because it is useful and enjoyable.
- **A reset can be productive.** Temporary work that provides routine, physical health, space, and free time can be a sound response to a bad work situation or burnout.

## Quotable versions

> There is no such thing as irrelevant experience.

> Build things and talk to people.

> Do things because why the fuck not.

> The rest will come.

> If the bossman starts backing away slowly, and you don’t seem to be following him: bail, hard and fast.

## How this applies to Semyon

For placement burnout: reduce application-agent churn temporarily; protect recovery; keep going to events and following up with people met there; maintain Oghma/startup work; reserve capacity for low-stakes, fun, technically varied builds. This is complementary to targeted placement applications, not a reason to abandon future applications entirely.
